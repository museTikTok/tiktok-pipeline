Try to tickle yourself right now. Go on.
Nothing, right?
But when someone else does it, you can't stop laughing.
Your brain is a spoiler. It ruins its own surprise.
Every time you move, your brain predicts what you're about to feel.
It sends a copy of the movement to your cerebellum, which predicts the touch and turns the volume down before it arrives.
But someone else's touch has no warning, so it hits at full blast.
Scientists proved it with a tickle machine. You move a lever, a robot tickles your palm — nothing.
Add a delay of just two-tenths of a second, and suddenly you can tickle yourself.
The prediction is wrong, so the brain stops cancelling the feeling.
And in one study, people with schizophrenia could tickle themselves — their prediction system doesn't cancel the touch.
Your brain doesn't feel the world. It guesses it.
And it can't surprise itself.
