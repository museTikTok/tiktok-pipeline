The sea vanished. Then it returned as a wall.
On December 26th, 2004, the ocean floor off Sumatra tore open in a magnitude 9.1 earthquake.
The shaking lasted almost ten minutes, the longest ever recorded.
In the deep ocean, the wave moved at the speed of a jet airliner.
Across the beaches of Thailand, the water pulled back, exposing the sea floor.
People walked out to look. Then the wave arrived.
In Khao Lak, a wall of water crashed over the beach, sweeping away everything in its path.
The water kept coming for hours, a black river of cars, trees, and concrete.
In Banda Aceh, waves up to thirty metres high erased whole neighbourhoods.
In Sri Lanka, the wave swept a crowded train off its tracks.
The tsunami raced across the Indian Ocean, reaching fourteen countries.
There was no warning system. No sirens. No time to run.
When the water went back, 227,898 people were dead.
227,898 people never came home.
