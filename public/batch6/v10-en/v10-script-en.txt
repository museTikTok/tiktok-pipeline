Thirty thousand people died in two minutes. Only two survived — and one of them was locked in a prison cell. This is Mount Pelée, the volcano that erased a city. Saint-Pierre was the Caribbean's richest city — the Paris of the Caribbean. But above it stood Mount Pelée, quiet for fifty years. In April 1902, the mountain woke up: ash, tremors, and sulphur in the air. Animals fled down the slopes, and a boiling mudflow buried a sugar factory. But officials told everyone to stay — an election was coming. On the morning of May 8th, at eight o'clock, the mountain exploded. A burning cloud raced down at a hundred and sixty kilometres an hour. It hit Saint-Pierre at eight oh two, and in two minutes, the whole city was gone. The only man left alive was Ludger Sylbaris, a prisoner locked in a windowless stone cell. The thick walls saved him — days later, rescuers heard him crying in the ruins. The second survivor was Léon Compère-Léandre, a shoemaker from the edge of the city. Saint-Pierre was never rebuilt — Fort-de-France took its place. When the volcano erupted again in 1929, everyone was evacuated, nobody died — follow for more stories history tried to bury!
