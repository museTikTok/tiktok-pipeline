Right now, you're flying east at 1,670 kilometres an hour — and you can't feel it. What if it stopped. Instantly.
The ground would stop. But you wouldn't. Everything not bolted to bedrock would keep flying east at 1,670 kilometres an hour.
You would NOT fly into space — gravity is nearly 300 times stronger than the spin.
Within seconds, it beats the strongest gust ever measured — 408 kilometres an hour, on Barrow Island. Six seconds in, hurricane force. By the end of a minute, faster than sound.
The oceans sweep east too — the biggest storm surge in history hits the west-facing shores, while eastern harbours drain away.
Nearly everyone would die. Then things would get interesting. The safest place? The poles — the ground there was barely moving.
But what if Earth just slowed down, gently? No chaos. Something stranger.
One day would last a whole year — six months of daylight, six months of night — and the Sun would rise in the west.
The oceans drain toward the poles, and one giant continent rises around the equator. In this model, London ends up 2.6 kilometres underwater.
No more hurricanes — and the magnetic field would weaken. Relax: Earth's day only gets 1.8 milliseconds longer per century. Would your city end up underwater, or on dry land?
